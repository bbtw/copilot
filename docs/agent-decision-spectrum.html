Agent Decision Spectrum

Financial Planning Co-Pilot. v1 design artifact, living document.

The agent is a synthesis layer on top of deterministic calculators and a firm-operated weighted multi-domain wellness model. Math and scoring stay auditable upstream; the LLM operates only on already-computed structured outputs. This document enumerates the decision points where we choose between deterministic behavior (rules, templates, wellness-driven ranking) and agent behavior (LLM judgment).

Every agent choice is a compliance surface area and an eval target. Every deterministic choice is a config/rules surface area. The split below is a proposal pending review, not a decision. Each row's classification is an opening position offered for the group to push back on.

Why the LLM half exists

The deterministic side gives the Brief its rigor: auditable Wellness Scores, a fixed inventory of upstream insights, rule-driven escalation. But rigor alone produces an artifact with a usability gap: a wall of per-domain scores, Calculator outputs, and bulleted insights that takes real effort to absorb. The LLM-flagged rows (#4, #5, #9) exist to close that gap.

For the Advisor

  • Synthesis across structured outputs. Wellness scores, calculator outputs, and insights arrive as separate data structures. Without the LLM, the Advisor cross-references them mentally every time. With it, they read a synthesis paragraph that names the dominant story up front.
    e.g., instead of scanning five wellness scores and four calculator outputs: "Retirement and tax are the two domains worth your attention this cycle. Cash flow, insurance, and estate are healthy and give you room to maneuver on the others."
  • Context-aware framing. The same calculator result (e.g., "retirement Monte Carlo: 72% success") reads very differently for a 35-year-old still accumulating vs. a 60-year-old five years from drawdown. Threshold-keyed templates collapse this; the LLM tailors the framing to the surrounding profile.
    e.g., for the 35-year-old: "72% is well within range to course-correct via savings rate over the next decade." For the 60-year-old: "72% understates sequence-of-returns risk; a bad first five years of drawdown has no time to recover."
  • Cross-cutting interactions. The deterministic side identifies issues in isolation. Only narrative can articulate how they interact. This is the highest-judgment part of reading the brief and the hardest to template.
    e.g., "The Roth conversion size that helps the tax picture also tightens next year's cash flow, and pushes AGI close to the IRMAA threshold; the three issues need to be sized together, not separately."
  • Lower friction onto the deterministic backing. A well-grounded synthesis names the scores and calculator outputs it's drawing from, so the Advisor reads the prose first and drills into the raw structured data when something looks off. The path from "open the brief" to "I know what to think about" shrinks from minutes to seconds.
    e.g., "Retirement (6/10) is driven by the 8% savings rate flagged in the retirement domain. See the retirement calculator output for the trajectory under the firm's 15% target."

The LLM does not add information beyond what the deterministic upstream produced. It makes that information usable inside the time the Advisor actually has.

Worked example: a wall of results, transformed

This is the transformation rows #4, #5, and #9 deliver end-to-end. The Advisor could read the deterministic outputs directly, but in practice, between meetings, will not. The example below uses a single hypothetical Client Profile; the structured "before" is what the deterministic layer produces, and the prose "after" is what the synthesis layer renders on top.

After · LLM synthesis

Retirement and tax are the two domains worth your attention this cycle. Cash flow, insurance, and estate are healthy and give you room to maneuver on the others.

Retirement (6/10) is driven by an 8% savings rate against the firm's 15% target. The 72% Monte Carlo success rate is in range, but the elevated first-five-year drawdown risk means a sequence-of-returns shock has limited time to recover. This is the more time-sensitive of the two.

The Roth conversion question is where the two intersect. Conversion headroom before the 2026 IRMAA threshold is ~$18,500; a larger conversion improves long-term tax positioning but crosses that threshold and tightens near-term cash flow even though cash flow is otherwise the client's strongest domain. The three levers (conversion size, savings rate, and cash flow) need to be sized together.

Grounding: retirement_monte_carlo (success_rate, first_5yr_drawdown_risk); tax_projection_2026 (IRMAA_threshold, roth_conversion_headroom); wellness ranking (retirement, tax as top two); insights (Roth conversion sizing, savings rate increase).

Nothing in the prose is new information. The dominant story (retirement and tax, meeting at the Roth conversion), the framing of the 72% as time-sensitive rather than reassuring, and the call-out that the three levers must be sized together: these reorganize the structured input into the form the Advisor will actually read.

What can still go wrong

Even with deterministic upstream and a narrowly-scoped synthesis role, the LLM can miss in ways that matter:

  • Number or domain hallucination. Citing a score, threshold, or domain that wasn't in the input (e.g., "estate scored 5/10" when the input said 8/10).
  • Ranking drift. Leading the prose with tax when the wellness ranking led with retirement, silently overriding row #3.
  • Tone drift. Slipping into directive language ("you should do the larger conversion") when co-pilot positioning requires tradeoff language ("the larger conversion improves X but costs Y").
  • Ungrounded cross-cutting claims. The example above asserts that the larger Roth conversion "tightens near-term cash flow." That's a defensible inference, but the deterministic layer didn't compute it. If the cash-flow calculator wasn't consulted, that sentence is synthesis-as-fact and could mislead.
  • Loss of grounding citations. Describing a Monte Carlo result without naming the calculator output, so the Advisor can't audit the claim or push back.

Mitigations

  • Per-row eval suites on #4, #5, #9. Each gates a specific failure mode: ranking fidelity (does prose lead with what wellness ranked first?), numerical fidelity (does every number in the prose appear in inputs?), tone compliance (no directive verbs).
  • Citation-style grounding. Synthesis emits per-claim references to the structured input it drew from, visible to the Advisor and machine-checkable in eval.
  • Cross-cutting inferences flagged as inference. When the agent claims an interaction not directly computed (e.g., the cash-flow tightening), the prose marks it as a synthesis call rather than a calculated fact, so the Advisor knows to verify or call the cash-flow calculator explicitly.
  • Advisor-in-loop as terminal safety net. The Brief is internal-only; the Advisor reviews before any client-facing use. This bounds blast radius but does not substitute for evals.
  • Conservative handling of missing inputs. When upstream flags a stale or missing input (row #11), synthesis is required to surface the gap explicitly rather than narrate around it.

Forward look: synthesis itself becomes agentic

v1 treats the LLM as a pure renderer: a fixed bundle of inputs goes in, prose comes out. The next step is to let the synthesis layer decide what else it needs mid-flow. Instead of running every calculator every time (row #1's current deterministic answer), the model would ingest wellness scores, identify the dominant story, and call additional calculators or query upstream insights on demand. For instance, noticing a possible IRMAA issue in the tax projection and then calling the cash-flow calculator to ground the cross-cutting claim that today's example only inferred.

That move expands agent surface area materially: row #1 ("which calculators to invoke") shifts from deterministic to agent, and every tool call becomes a new eval surface and audit question. It is not v1 work. But the v1 architecture (deterministic calculators with structured outputs, advisor-in-loop, eval coverage on the synthesis rows) is the foundation that makes that move tractable later. Today's "run everything, every time" posture trades compute for compliance simplicity; the agentic-synthesis posture trades the other way, and is worth taking only once the synthesis layer's failure modes are understood well enough to bound the larger surface area.

Upstream dependency: the firm's wellness model

Rows #3, #4 (partial), #6, and #11 are anchored in the firm's existing weighted multi-domain wellness model. This is firm infrastructure, governed and compliance-reviewed outside this project. The agent reads its output; it does not compute scores, decide weights, or override its rankings.

This is the architectural anchor that lets us push ranking, issue-identification, and gap-detection out of the LLM. The regulatory surface area for those decisions lives in the wellness model (already a load-bearing firm artifact) instead of inside the agent.

Open questions about the wellness model's output

The exact split for rows #4 (narrative) and #5 (numerical interpretation) depends on what the wellness model emits today. Specifically:

  • Drivers / explanations. Does each wellness score come with structured "why this score" data (e.g., "retirement = 6/10 because savings rate is 8% vs target 15%"), or only the bare score? Drivers are the LLM's grounding source for narrative; without them, prose risks being ungrounded.
  • Ranking output. Does the model emit a pre-ranked list, or only per-domain scores leaving the ranking logic (largest gap? lowest score? weighted?) to be implemented as a thin deterministic node?
  • Score scale and semantics. What range, and what does "good" vs "concerning" mean numerically and clinically?

These TBDs do not change which rows are deterministic vs agent, but they shape how much grounding the agent has when narrating (row #5 in particular).

Open questions about insights

Insights are a new upstream artifact (introduced by rows #7 and #8). Their shape affects whether row #9 (tradeoff phrasing) stays purely agent-side or splits into deterministic content with agent rendering:

  • Tradeoff packaging. Do pre-calculated insights carry their own tradeoff descriptions (axes + framings), or does the agent derive tradeoffs from insight + calculator outputs? If they carry tradeoffs, row #9 becomes content-deterministic with agent rendering (same split pattern as row #4). If not, row #9 stays fully agent.

How to read this document

  • Rows currently marked Deterministic are proposed to be implemented as rules, fixed configs, or templated outputs; no LLM call.
  • Rows currently marked Agent are proposed to be handled by an LLM node and will need eval coverage.
  • The "Proposed" column reflects an initial split designed to minimize agent surface area while still giving the LLM the synthesis/writing work it's good at.
  • This document will evolve as we work through the design, including both the rows themselves and the split.
